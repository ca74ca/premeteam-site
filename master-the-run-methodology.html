Master The Run™

Build The Pace

The Run is half your race, and the part most people forget to train. Athletes sprint the first kilometer on adrenaline and spend the next seven paying for it. Master The Run develops aerobic base, pacing discipline, and the ability to run well on tired legs so every kilometer feels controlled, not desperate. The race isn't won on fresh legs. It's won on the eighth kilometer. Build The Pace.

What it is

Between every station you run about 1 km: 8 runs, 8,000 meters in total. You run before the first station and after each of the first seven, then finish with the eighth station (Wall Balls).

The race standard (all divisions: 8 × ~1 km = 8,000 m)

• The run course totals 8,000 m, split into 8 segments of about 1,000 m. Venues often use laps, so count them and follow the course map. • There's a Fast Lane on the inside for racers running about 4:00 per km or faster. Everyone else uses the wider Running Lane. • Enter and exit stations through the correct arches. Coming out through the "IN" arch is a 2-minute penalty. • Walking is allowed. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 7.2; hyrox.com/the-fitness-race.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Most of your running should be easy. That means Zone 2, or "conversation pace": you could talk in full sentences. It feels too slow at first. That's the point; it builds your engine without wrecking you. 2. Run/walk is a legitimate method. Start with intervals like 2 min jog / 1 min walk and slowly shrink the walks. 3. Practise "compromised running". That's running straight after a station, when your legs feel heavy. Do it once a week, for example 10 lunges then 400 m jog. 4. Increase slowly. Add no more than about 10% to your weekly running each week. 5. Learn your goal pace. Run 1 km at a pace you could repeat 8 times. Time it. That's your race pace.

Common mistakes

• Running the first 1 km way too fast because of the crowd and the music. • Every run being a hard run (no easy days means slow progress and more injuries). • Overstriding (heel slamming way out in front). Take shorter, quicker steps instead. • Never practising running on tired legs. • Losing count of laps in the venue.

Scaled options

• Run/walk intervals (1:1, then 2:1, then 3:1). • Treadmill with a 1% incline if outdoor running is hard on your joints. • Bike or rower for some easy sessions while your legs adapt.

Sample beginner session (about 35 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): 5 min brisk walk, then leg swings, 10 walking lunges, 3 × 20-sec relaxed strides. • Main set (22 min): 6 × (3 min easy jog at conversation pace + 1 min walk) + 4 min easy walk-jog. • Cool-down (5 min): walk, then calf and hip stretches. • Week-by-week: each week, add 30 sec to the jog or cut 15 sec from the walk.
