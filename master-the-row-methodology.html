Master The Row™

Build The Rhythm

The Row is where the second half of your race begins. Most athletes pull with their arms, rush the slide, and walk off the rower with burning legs and a racing heart. Master The Row teaches the leg-driven stroke, a calm stroke rate, and pacing control so you finish the 1,000 meters ready to run. Long and strong beats short and frantic. Build The Rhythm.

What it is

Station 5. 1,000 meters on an indoor rowing machine (an "erg"). Good rowing is about 60% legs, 20% core and 20% arms, so it's a full-body cardio test.

The race standard (all divisions: 1,000 m)

• The judges reset the monitor before you start. • You must stay seated until all 1,000 m are done and a judge confirms it. You may stop and rest on the seat. • The damper (resistance lever) is preset to 6 and the footplates to position 4. You can adjust both. • Penalties: a warning for the first infringement, then +15 seconds for each one after that. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 8.5.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Learn the order: legs → body → arms on the way out, then arms → body → legs on the way back. Push with your legs first, swing your body back slightly, then pull the handle to the bottom of your ribs. 2. Slow the return. The recovery slide should take about twice as long as the drive. 3. Watch two numbers: stroke rate (s/m, strokes per minute) and split (/500 m pace). Beginners aim for roughly 22–26 strokes per minute. 4. Strap in properly. The strap goes across the widest part of your foot. 5. Practise the run straight after. Row 500 m, then jog 400 m, so your legs learn the switch.

Common mistakes

• Pulling with the arms before the legs have finished pushing. • Rushing up the slide (high stroke rate, little power). • Cranking the damper to 10 thinking it's "harder = better". It just tires your legs. • Going out at sprint pace for the first 200 m. • Hunching your back at the front of the stroke.

Scaled options

• Shorter pieces: 4 × 250 m with rest, building toward 1,000 m continuous. • Lower damper (4–5) while learning. • No rower? Use a bike or assault bike for the same time, plus seated band rows for the pulling pattern.

Sample beginner session (about 40 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): 5 min easy row (rate 20, RPE 3), then 3 × 10 strokes legs-only, 10 strokes legs + body, 10 full strokes. • Main set (24 min): 4 rounds of 500 m row at RPE 6 (rate 22–26) + 400 m easy jog. Rest 90 sec. • Cool-down (5 min): easy walk, then a hamstring and upper-back stretch.
