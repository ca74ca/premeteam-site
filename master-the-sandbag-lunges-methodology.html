Master The Sandbag Lunges™

Build The Durability

The Sandbag Lunges are where legs go to be tested. Athletes rush the first lanes, lose their balance, and grind through the rest one painful step at a time. Master The Sandbag Lunges builds leg strength, balance, and a steady rhythm so you cover 100 meters with control and still have legs for the run and the wall. Steady steps cover ground. Build The Durability.

What it is

Station 7. With a sandbag resting on your shoulders, you walking-lunge 100 meters. It's mostly thighs and glutes, with your core working hard to keep you upright, and it comes right before the final run and the Wall Balls.

The race standard (100 m)

Women (Open): 10 kg (≈ 22 lb) Men (Open) / Women Pro: 20 kg (≈ 44 lb) Men Pro: 30 kg (≈ 66 lb) • Your back knee must clearly touch the ground on every lunge, and you must stand tall (hips and knees straight) at the end of each rep. • Lunges alternate legs. You can lunge continuously or pause with both feet together, but no extra steps or shuffles. • Your front foot must fully cross each lane's end line and the finish line. • The sandbag must stay supported by you; resting it on the ground, barriers or your feet is +15 seconds each time. Return it to the marked area (otherwise +30 seconds). Using the wrong weight means repeating the station. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 8.7.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Bodyweight first. Do walking lunges with no weight. Take a comfortable step, lower your back knee gently to the floor (use a mat), and keep your front heel down. 2. Then add load. Use a light sandbag, or a dumbbell held at your chest (goblet). Build to race weight over several weeks. 3. Learn to carry the bag. Rest it across your upper back and shoulders, behind your neck, held with both hands. 4. Find your rhythm. Choose a steady pace, for example 10 lunges then a 5-second pause standing tall, and repeat. 5. Build leg strength twice a week: split squats, step-ups and glute bridges.

Common mistakes

• Not touching the back knee down (no-rep). • Steps that are too short (more reps) or too long (loss of balance). Find your middle. • Leaning forward and letting the bag pull you over. • Shuffling your feet between reps, which isn't allowed. • Going too fast in the first lane, then stopping every few reps.

Scaled options

• Bodyweight walking lunges. • Lighter sandbag or a single dumbbell held at the chest. • Reverse lunges in place (easier on the knees) to build strength first. • Shorter distance: 4 × 25 m with rest, building toward 100 m.

Sample beginner session (about 35 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): 5 min easy bike or walk, 10 glute bridges, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 reverse lunges. • Strength (10 min): 3 × 8 split squats each leg (bodyweight or light dumbbells), rest 60 sec. • Main set (12 min): 4 rounds of 20 m walking lunges (light sandbag or bodyweight, RPE 6) + 200 m easy jog. • Cool-down (5 min): quad, hip flexor and glute stretches.
