Master The Wall Balls™

Build The Precision

The Wall Balls stand between you and the finish line. Tired legs, shallow squats, and missed targets turn 100 reps into 130. Master The Wall Balls develops squat depth, a consistent throw, and smart rep sets so you can hit every target and finish the race strong. Every rep that counts brings you closer. Build The Precision.

What it is

Station 8, the last one. You hold a medicine ball (the "wall ball") at your chest, squat down, and as you stand up you throw it to a target high on a wall. Catch it, and repeat 100 times. Then you're done.

The race standard (100 reps)

Women (Open): 4 kg (≈ 9 lb) ball, 2.70 m (≈ 8 ft 10 in) target Women Pro: 6 kg (≈ 13 lb) ball, 2.70 m target Men (Open): 6 kg (≈ 13 lb) ball, 3.00 m (≈ 9 ft 10 in) target Men Pro: 9 kg (≈ 20 lb) ball, 3.00 m target • Start each rep standing tall, holding the ball with both hands. You can't pick it up off the floor and throw straight away. • Squat until your hips are lower than your knees, then throw with both hands and hit your target zone. • You can catch the ball, or let it hit the floor, but you can't catch it after a bounce and keep going. • No warnings: every bad rep is a "no rep", and each missing rep is +15 seconds. Powdered chalk at this station is a 2-minute penalty. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 8.8.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Master the squat first: feet shoulder-width, chest up, sit back and down until your hips go below your knees. Use a box behind you to learn the depth. 2. Practise the throw with a light ball. Hold the ball at your chin with elbows under it. Stand up fast and let your legs launch the ball; your arms just guide it. 3. Catch and sink in one move. Catch the ball at chin height and go straight down into the next squat. 4. Break it up on purpose. Beginners do best with planned sets, for example 10 sets of 10, with 3–5 breaths of rest between, instead of going until failure. 5. Practise on tired legs: wall balls straight after lunges or a run, once a week.

Common mistakes

• Not squatting deep enough (the most common no-rep). • Throwing with the arms instead of the legs, which burns out your shoulders. • Standing too far from the wall (aim for about an arm's length). • Going unbroken until failure, then needing long rests. • Looking down. Keep your eyes on the target.

Scaled options

• Lighter ball (2–3 kg) or a lower target while you learn. • Squat to a box or bench for depth. • Air squats + overhead ball press (no throw) to build the pattern. • Fewer reps: 50 total in sets of 5, building toward 100.

Sample beginner session (about 35 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): 5 min easy row or bike, 10 air squats, 10 squats with a 3-sec pause at the bottom, 10 light wall ball throws. • Skill (7 min): 5 × 5 wall balls focusing on depth and a smooth catch, 30 sec rest. • Main set (15 min): 5 rounds of 10–15 wall balls (RPE 6) + 200 m jog. Rest 60 sec. • Cool-down (5 min): quad and shoulder stretches, easy walk.
