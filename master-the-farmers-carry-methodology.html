Master The Farmers Carry™

Build The Stability

The Farmers Carry looks simple. That's the trap. Grip fades, shoulders creep up, and every extra put-down costs time you won't get back. Master The Farmers Carry develops grip endurance, posture, and walking rhythm so you can carry the load from start to finish with fewer stops. Strong hands. Quiet feet. Build The Stability.

What it is

Station 6. Pick up two kettlebells, one in each hand, and walk 200 meters. It works your grip, shoulders, upper back and core. It also wakes up your legs right before the lunges.

The race standard (200 m)

Women (Open): 2 × 16 kg (≈ 35 lb each) Men (Open) / Women Pro: 2 × 24 kg (≈ 53 lb each) Men Pro: 2 × 32 kg (≈ 70 lb each) • Carry both kettlebells at all times while you move, arms straight at your sides. • You may put them down to rest, as long as they don't move forward when you do. • Return them to the marked box, with handles upright, when you finish (otherwise +30 seconds). Each missing lap is +3 minutes, and using the wrong weight means repeating the station. • Depending on the venue, 200 m may be several laps. Check the athlete map. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 8.6.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Start lighter than race weight. Begin with a weight you can carry 50 m without stopping, using dumbbells or kettlebells. 2. Posture first: stand tall, shoulders down and back, ribs down, eyes forward. Imagine "proud chest, long neck". 3. Short, quick steps. Walk briskly heel-to-toe. Don't shuffle and don't overstride. 4. Build grip strength: dead hangs from a bar and holds of heavy dumbbells for time, twice a week. 5. Progress one thing at a time: first distance (50 → 100 → 200 m), then weight.

Common mistakes

• Shoulders shrugging up toward the ears, which tires your grip and neck fast. • Leaning sideways or letting the bells swing. • Waiting until your grip completely fails before setting down. A planned 5-second put-down is faster than a dropped bell and a long rest. • Running with the bells (wasted energy and a dropping risk).

Scaled options

• Lighter dumbbells or kettlebells (for example 8–12 kg each for women, 12–16 kg for men while building). • Shorter carries: 4 × 50 m with planned rests. • Single-arm "suitcase" carries to build core strength. • Use hex-bar or trap-bar carries if available (easier to grip).

Sample beginner session (about 35 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): 5 min easy row or bike, 10 band pull-aparts, 10 goblet squats with a light weight. • Main set (20 min): 4 rounds of 2 × 50 m farmers carry (RPE 6, with a 30 sec rest between carries) + 400 m easy run or walk. • Grip finisher (4 min): 3 × 20-sec dead hang from a bar (or hold heavy dumbbells), rest 40 sec. • Cool-down (3 min): forearm and upper trap stretches.
