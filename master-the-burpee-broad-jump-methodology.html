Master The Burpee Broad Jump™

Build The Control

The Burpee Broad Jump is where impatience gets expensive. Most athletes jump too far, too early, and turn 80 meters into a fight for air. Master The Burpee Broad Jump builds rhythm, breathing, and repeatable jump distance so you can move steadily from start line to finish line. Small and steady beats big and broken. Build The Control.

What it is

Station 4. You do a burpee (chest to the floor, then back up), then jump forward with both feet, land, and go straight into the next burpee. You keep going until you've covered 80 meters. It's a full-body move: legs, chest, shoulders and a lot of breathing.

The race standard (all divisions: 80 m)

• Every burpee: your chest must clearly touch the ground (the rulebook defines this as the nipple line making contact). • You may step or jump back into the burpee and step or jump out of it, and you may use a knee to get up. Your feet can't land beyond your fingertips when you come up. • Hands go down no more than 30 cm in front of your front foot's toes. • Every broad jump: both feet take off and land together (no more than 5 cm stagger), with no extra steps or shuffles. • Penalties: a warning for the first infringement, then +15 seconds for each one after that. Source: HYROX Singles Rulebook 26/27, section 8.4.

How to train it as a beginner

1. Learn the burpee first. Practise a step-back burpee: hands down, step one foot back then the other, lower your chest to the floor, push up, step in, stand. Smooth beats fast. 2. Learn a short, soft jump. Jump forward about one shoe-length and land quietly, with knees bent and both feet together. 3. Join them up. Do sets of 5 burpee broad jumps, rest, repeat. Focus on the same rhythm every rep. 4. Breathe on purpose. Breathe out as you go down and in as you stand. Find a pattern and stick to it. 5. Build distance over weeks: 20 m → 40 m → 60 m → 80 m without stopping for long.

Common mistakes

• Jumping as far as possible. Big jumps cost huge energy and make the next burpee harder. • Hands landing too far ahead to "gain distance" (no-rep risk and wasted effort). • Not getting the chest all the way down. • Holding your breath, then having to stop and stand still. • Sprinting the first 20 m and walking the last 60.

Scaled options (easier versions)

• Step-back burpee + step-forward instead of a jump. • Burpee to an incline (hands on a box or bench) + small jump. • Fewer reps: 10 m sections with rest, building toward 80 m. • If burpees hurt your wrists or shoulders: squat thrusts or "sprawls" to a bench while you build strength.

Sample beginner session (about 35 min)

• Warm-up (8 min): easy walk/jog 4 min, then 10 air squats, 10 inchworms, 10 small two-foot jumps. • Skill (7 min): 3 × 5 slow burpees, focusing on a smooth chest-to-floor, 1 min rest. • Main set (15 min): 5 rounds of 8 burpee broad jumps (RPE 6) + 200 m easy jog or brisk walk. Rest 60 sec. • Cool-down (5 min): walk, then a hip flexor and chest stretch.
